**[product] intro_product**
This is the Apple iPhone Duo. It starts at $1,999 for 256 gigabytes of storage, and climbs all the way to $3,199 if you max it out with two terabytes. That makes it the most expensive iPhone Apple has ever built.

**[timeline] timeline_rd**
Apple didn't rush into this form factor overnight. In fact, John Ternus filed patents for folding displays back in 2011, spending fifteen years refining hinge mechanisms before putting this into your hands.

**[specs] specs_displays**
On the outside, you get a 5.4-inch Super Retina XDR cover screen. Unfold the passport-style chassis, and you reveal a 7.6-inch tablet display that peaks at 3,000 nits outdoors.

**[process] process_crease**
The engineering feat everyone is talking about is the crease, or rather the lack of one. Apple stacks ten ultrathin layers with a stiff polymer cover, supported by under-display glass and custom gliding adhesives that absorb bending stress whenever you open it.

**[quote] press_quote**
Early hands-on reviews praise the build quality. PCMag called it the best first-gen foldable they've used, noting that the matte nano-texture finish virtually hides the seam under ambient light.

**[list] compromise_list**
But achieving this profile demanded significant trade-offs. You do not get Face ID here. Instead, Apple brought back Touch ID inside the side power button, which feels surprising on a flagship in 2026.

**[stat] repair_stat**
And you really do not want to drop this device. If you damage the inner folding display without AppleCare Plus, an out-of-warranty screen replacement costs $999. That is half the price of the phone itself.

**[screenshot] screenshot_source**
Checking the official announcement, Apple points to a 3D-printed micro-blasted titanium hinge cover housing over one hundred components, paired with an IP68 dust and water resistance rating.

**[process] process_cameras**

**[compare] compare_buying**
If you are trying to buy one on launch day, keep expectations in check. Apple Store walk-ins are virtually zero due to manufacturing yield bottlenecks, meaning carrier financing might be your only shot at getting a unit before the holidays.

Here is the honest verdict. The iPhone Duo proves Apple can engineer a folding hinge better than anyone else, but paying over two grand for gen-one sacrifices and a three-month backorder isn't practical. Let early adopters test the waters while you wait for round two.
